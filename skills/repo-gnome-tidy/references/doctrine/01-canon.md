# Canon states the present

Canon says what is, what it is for and what to do. It does not say how a
rule came to be, what it replaced, who decided it or when. That history
belongs in a record that does not change; canon is tended, and what it no
longer needs leaves it.

**CANON-1. Present tense.** A canon document says what is, what it is for,
how it works and what to do, in the present tense. A reason stays when it
changes what the reader does. A limit stays when it affects use, stated
once, where the reader meets it.

## What leaves

**CANON-2. Dated ruling.** A sentence that says who decided a rule and when.
"The maintainer ruled this on 2025-03-02." "Decided on 2 March 2025 that
the server is built in a repository of its own." The rule stays; the
decider and the date leave. A date stays where the reader needs it to do
the work, such as the date a record was sealed under.

**CANON-3. Ghost of the alternative.** A rule written as the negation of a
proposal that the document does not otherwise mention. "There is no
registry file." "No wrapper directory is needed." "These need not be five
files." "Do not infer a commitment from the presence of design notes." The
reader was not going to make the registry, the wrapper or the inference,
and the negation tells them nothing the positive statement does not. The
positive statement stays, once.

**CANON-4. Scar rule.** A prohibition exactly as wide as one past incident.
"Do not rewrite the journal with a script." "Do not create a dummy intray
entry to test approvals." "Before moving a scope, read the handover of 9
March for what depended on a path." The incident is in the record that was
sealed; canon holds the general rule the incident was a case of, which is
usually already there, or the tool that makes the mistake impossible, or
nothing.

**CANON-5. Same rule, many homes.** One rule restated in several documents.
Each copy drifts from the others, and a reader who finds one does not know
whether it is the one that is maintained. A rule has one home. Another
document that needs it links to the home or says nothing.

**CANON-6. Register as a second copy.** A register of rules that restates
each rule beside a link to its home, and machinery that keeps the copies
aligned. The register holds for each rule its id, its home and what checks
it; the restatement leaves, since the home has it.

**CANON-7. Status in canon.** A sentence that says where work stands: "not
yet", "planned", "none yet", "is not built", "has been used once", "remains
to be observed", a section of open questions or gaps. Status goes to a
task, with an owner and the occasion it returns on. Canon says what the
thing is.

**CANON-8. Quoted authority.** The maintainer's words quoted as the warrant
for a rule: "In the maintainer's words, every producer has a review loop."
The rule stays, stated plainly. A quotation stays only where it is the
clearest statement of the idea, without the frame that says whose it is or
when it was said.

**CANON-9. Running log.** A section of a present-tense page that grows by a
paragraph each time something happens, newest first, each paragraph a
summary of a record kept elsewhere. The page says in one sentence where
the records are and how they are found. What the log held that is still
open is a task.

**CANON-10. Notes to the writer.** Instructions about the document itself,
addressed to whoever tends it next: "Using this document", "Learn from
use", "Refine this skill from observed use", "Line count alone is not the
measure of order". The document is for its reader. How documents are
reviewed and refined is said once, where the review loops are named.

**CANON-11. Layered carve-outs.** A rule followed by its exceptions, each
with its own degree of force: "allowed but poor form", "allowed but not
advisable", "forbidden except within a collection". The cases are a table
with one row each, saying what to do in that case. Degrees of force leave
with the prose.

## What stays

Present-tense statements of what a thing is, what it is for, how it works,
what to do and when. The reasons that change what the reader does. Where
things are. What is open now, in the one place status belongs, as a task.
A real limit that affects use, stated once. A link to a record the reader
opens while doing the work: a specimen, a fixture, a dataset, a
specification. Task bodies, which may hold state and quotations where
canon prose does not.

oneself, aphorism and tails of the form "X, not Y" are the same faults in
canon, and a canon policy may list them beside these clauses.
